- (event) MEASURED OA300 tempo distribution (82 labelled tracks): <100 = 18 (clustered 80-85), 100-120 = 1 (single track at 102), 120-140 = 7, 140-160 = 15, 160-175 = 41, 175+ = 0. Corpus is MORE DnB-skewed than the training set; half of it sits in one band. Three consequences: (a) the 100-120 gap cannot be sourced from OA300; (b) the 18 tracks at 80-85 and 41 at 160-175 are the SAME tempo octave annotated at different metrical levels - the corpus encodes both halves of the octave ambiguity as ground truth, a plausible mechanism for 'when in doubt, fast' and directly testable; (c) the 55/82 Acc1 floor is substantially a measurement of 160 BPM performance.

Open item: 100-120 material must be sourced elsewhere.
- (change) RESEARCH INVALIDATES MY OWN ASSUMPTION AS-6: TempoCNN does NOT use Gaussian smearing. Schreiber & Muller ISMIR 2018 uses plain categorical cross-entropy on ONE-HOT targets over 256 bins - identical to ours. 'Add Gaussian smoothing because Schreiber does' is folklore and false. Bock ISMIR 2019 does smear but TRIANGULAR, +/-2 BPM, neighbours 0.5/0.25 (~2 bins not ~8), and never ablates it. No tempo paper ablates smearing vs one-hot or ablates sigma. General ordinal evidence IS real and same-architecture (DLDL TIP 2017 MAE 2.51 vs one-hot 3.02 vs UNIFORM smoothing 2.96 - ordinal structure is the active ingredient, not smoothing per se; Imani & White ICML 2018 HL-Gaussian 8.99 vs HL-OneBin 28.00).
- (event) RESEARCH (highest-value finding of the whole Discovery): Horschlager, Vogl, Bock & Knees, SMC 2015 'Addressing Tempo Estimation Octave Errors in Electronic Music' - genre/style-conditioned tempo priors lift DnB Acc1 from 7.19% to 78.42% (overall 45.5% -> 75.0%) on GiantSteps, which is 20% DnB with a DnB prior of 130-180 BPM. This is directly on this project's problem, needs no model, and is the same idea already filed as #172 (consumer-specifiable BPM search range / tempo prior). Promoted to a top-ranked feature.
- (event) RESEARCH: NO published tempo system places octave-partner mass in the TRAINING TARGET. Our 0.15 split across {2T, T/2} has zero published precedent, symmetric or otherwise. Published practice handles octaves at DECODE (Morais 2024: three highest peaks, pick the middle if they form a half/double relation) or via asymmetric style-conditioned PRIORS (SMC 2015). Also: expectation-decoding is CONTRAINDICATED for tempo (posteriors are multimodal across octaves; expectation over 87 and 174 yields ~130, a tempo in neither mode) - Bock's local quadratic interpolation is the safe middle.
