- (change) RESEARCH INVALIDATES MY OWN ASSUMPTION AS-6: TempoCNN does NOT use Gaussian smearing. Schreiber & Muller ISMIR 2018 uses plain categorical cross-entropy on ONE-HOT targets over 256 bins - identical to ours. 'Add Gaussian smoothing because Schreiber does' is folklore and false. Bock ISMIR 2019 does smear but TRIANGULAR, +/-2 BPM, neighbours 0.5/0.25 (~2 bins not ~8), and never ablates it. No tempo paper ablates smearing vs one-hot or ablates sigma. General ordinal evidence IS real and same-architecture (DLDL TIP 2017 MAE 2.51 vs one-hot 3.02 vs UNIFORM smoothing 2.96 - ordinal structure is the active ingredient, not smoothing per se; Imani & White ICML 2018 HL-Gaussian 8.99 vs HL-OneBin 28.00).

- (decision) Q11 settled 2026-07-29 via Codex consult, every figure verified independently. A per-band noninferiority test is NOT attainable at n=43 and writing one would be theatre: zero-margin needs near-unanimous discordance (d=4 impossible at any split, d=7 needs 7:0, d=9 needs 8:1), and even with zero observed losses the exact one-sided 97.5% upper bound on the loss rate is 1-0.025^(1/43) = 8.2 percent = 3.54 track-equivalents, so perfect data rules out four tracks and cannot rule out three. Ruling out a one-track margin would need about 157 tracks per band, a two-track margin 78. Adopted instead: a deterministic benchmark tripwire. Predeclare DSP-handled bands from the locked DSP-only baseline at 30-of-43 correct, require gains >= losses in each, and any failing band blocks approval pending documented track-level review and a recorded waiver - scoped to the benchmark, never claimed as population noninferiority. Three riders: the review needs an owner and a recorded waiver or it is theatre too; add a system-by-band heterogeneity test as a second tripwire since it would have caught the +32/-16 case at p below 1e-6 but is not a substitute; report gross losses separately from net because a net-zero band can swap many previously-correct tracks.
