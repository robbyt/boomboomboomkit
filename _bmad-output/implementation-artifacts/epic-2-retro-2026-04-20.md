## Successes

- **Scalable benchmark infrastructure** that tracks both wall-clock and accuracy in immutable per-run data files. This is the primary deliverable -- ensures accuracy is never compromised as the library evolves. robbyt identified this as the most important outcome of the epic.
- **Zero accuracy regression** across 6 stories of infrastructure surgery. OA300 Acc1=57/82 (69.5%), Acc2=73/82 (89.0%) held bit-identical from start to finish. GiantSteps improved from Acc1=70.0% to 81.1% and Acc2=79.1% to 82.5% via correctness fixes (dual-tempo matching, MIREX-compliant Acc2 factors).
- **Structural separation** of benchmark and unit test targets. `make test` runs clean in seconds; corpus-gated benchmarks live in a separate target that can't accidentally activate. This decision in Story 2-3 paid dividends in every subsequent story.
- **File-per-run baseline redesign** (Story 2-6) simplified away the complexity of Story 2-3's staging/mutex/perf-commit.sh design. Each run is an immutable JSON file, trivially diffable in git. The simplest design won by iterating through the complex design first.
- **Genre-stratified reporting** identified 4 GiantSteps LOW-flagged genres (house 52.2%, progressive-house 57.9%, electro-house 68.2%, dj-tools 12.5%) -- the first actionable tuning data for Epic 3.
- **Near-perfect follow-through on Epic 1 retro commitments** (5/6 completed). Previous Story Intelligence sections, deterministic async patterns, standard gating checklist, multi-model review, and tighter task scoping all carried forward successfully. F6 deferred debt intentionally left for Epic 3.
- **Stories got cleaner as the epic progressed.** 2-1/2-2 were tight (3 patches, 0 decision-needed each). 2-3 was heavy (4 review passes). 2-4/2-5/2-6 found a rhythm (1-2 rounds each). Infrastructure epics are front-loaded by nature.

## Challenges

- **Story 2-3 scope grew significantly mid-flight.** The v2 schema revision, test-target structural separation, and accuracy snapshot feature were all discovered during implementation rather than surfaced for Project Lead review beforehand. The story spec was 500+ lines; the critical schema design decision was buried deep. robbyt noted: "I should have reviewed the v1 schema before you started, but I was lost in all the detail and planning."
- **Spec-code drift was the #1 review theme.** Stories 2-3 and 2-6 had multiple findings where AC text, task text, or dev notes diverged from shipped code after mid-story changes. In Story 2-3 alone, ACs #1, #3, #4, and #5 had to be rewritten to match what was actually built.
- **Model switch introduced a confounding variable.** Opus 4.7 was tested during Stories 2-3 through 2-5 over the weekend. robbyt switched back to Opus 4.6 for better performance. Some of the review churn attributed to planning problems may have been tooling problems -- a less spec-disciplined model producing more review findings, which compounds into more patches and more spec-code drift.
- **Pre-existing DAW oracle failure persists.** The `DAWOracleTrack` CodingKeys + `.convertFromSnakeCase` conflict was documented in Story 2-4 and confirmed as pre-existing. Not fixed during Epic 2. Blocks Story 3-2 in Epic 3.

## Key Insights

1. **Scalable measurement infrastructure is the foundation.** Wall-clock + accuracy in immutable per-run files, tracked over time, ensures accuracy is never compromised. Everything else in the project depends on trusting the numbers.
2. **Surface critical design decisions early.** Big stories need a "Key Design Decisions" section at the top so the Project Lead can review load-bearing choices before dev begins. Detail is good; burying the important decisions in detail is not.
3. **Model choice matters for precision work.** Opus 4.6 is the better fit for spec-disciplined infrastructure stories. Model experiments should happen on low-risk stories or branches, not on load-bearing infrastructure mid-epic.
4. **Previous Story Intelligence is a proven force multiplier.** Every story in Epic 2 referenced prior findings. Story 2-2 closed the Acc2 divergence 2-1 explicitly deferred. Story 2-4's genre decode guard came directly from 2-3's shared-types work. This pattern should continue indefinitely.
