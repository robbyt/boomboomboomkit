# Investigation: Phase 0.5 accuracy ceiling sweep — which lever (octave-decision vs candidate-generation) caps BPM accuracy

## Hand-off Brief

1. **What happened.** The Phase-0 forensic harness (`accuracy-forensics-{oa300,giantsteps}.json`) shows OA300 BPM accuracy is bottlenecked by **octave error**, not candidate generation: 16 of 24 OA300 misses are a clean ×2/×0.5 octave, and in 15/16 a harmonic of the true tempo is the *top-scored* candidate. *(Confirmed.)* The original mechanism read — that a winner-vs-truth `scoreMargin ≈ 0` proves "a periodicity tie broken the wrong way" by `resolveOctaveAmbiguity` — is **UNCONFIRMED** (the margin is tautological for octave rows; only one track showed a true resolver flip — Follow-up 2026-06-27), and the fix it implied is **REJECTED**: every raw-periodicity demote variant regressed both corpora (Follow-up 2026-06-28). The lever is octave error, but NOT via demoting periodicity candidates.
2. **Where the case stands.** OA300 octave-decision ceiling = 74/82; GiantSteps not octave-bound. **The first fix attempt is REJECTED (2026-06-28 Follow-up):** un-gating `resolveOctaveAmbiguity` to make the sub-band vote authoritative regressed BOTH corpora (OA300 58→39/40, GS 537→503/507) and was reverted. Confirmed root cause: the faster-only ratchet is LOAD-BEARING compensation for ACF subharmonic bias (the slow octave autocorrelates ≥ the fundamental) — it is not a bug. A unanimous codex+agy+literature quorum was empirically wrong and conceded.
3. **What's needed next.** The entire DEMOTE-the-periodicity-candidate class is exhausted (4 variants, all neutral-to-negative — see the 2026-06-28 Follow-up). Next octave lever must NOT trust raw periodicity: (1) perceptual ~120 BPM resonance prior as a tiebreak, (2) beat-grid coherence (W53/W74), (3) learned octave classifier (Epic 12). See the Follow-up for data, citations, and rationale.

## Problem Statement

The external-audit roadmap's stated highest-leverage lever is "octave/half-double disambiguation on OA300-like material." Phase 0.5 must confirm — from data, before any DSP change — whether OA300's accuracy ceiling is **selection/decision-bound** (the true tempo is reachable but the pipeline picks the wrong octave) or **generation-bound** (the true tempo is never surfaced), and pick the Phase-1 target accordingly.

## Confirmed Findings

### Finding 1: OA300 misses are dominated by octave error (16/24)

**Evidence:** `accuracy-forensics-oa300.json` → `errorTypeHistogram` = `{exact:58, double:11, half:5, twoThird:2, other:6}`.

**Detail:** Of 24 misses, 11 are ×2 (true ~80–86 BPM jungle/dnb doubled to ~160–172), 5 are ×0.5 (true 160 halved to ~80), 2 triplet (2:3), 6 non-harmonic "other". Octave = 16/24 = **67% of OA300 failures**.

### Finding 2: A truth harmonic is the top candidate in 15/16 octave misses; only 3/24 are generation-bound

**Evidence:** Per-track `recall` for the 16 octave misses — `bestRank == 1` in 15/16. `recallSplit` = `{missTrueAtRank1:17, missTrueInTop5:21, missTrueAbsent:3}`. `scoreMargin` defined `winnerScore − oracleCandidateScore` (`AccuracyForensics.swift:65,199`).

**Detail:** A harmonic of the true tempo is the top-scored candidate in 15/16 octave misses; only **3/24** misses are generation-bound (no truth harmonic in candidates: `Wind It Up`, `9. HEFT`, `Smalltown Boy`).

**Margin caveat (Codex cross-check, 2026-06-27 — corrects an earlier overclaim):** `scoreMargin ≈ 0` does **not** evidence a true-vs-wrong-octave score tie. `winnerScore` is the candidate nearest the final `detectedBPM`; for a `matchedFactor=="2x"/"0.5x"` row the truth-matching candidate *is itself* in the wrong-octave band (≈ the winner), so `margin ≈ 0` is largely tautological. Margin distribution: `0.5x` 5/5 rows margin 0; `2x` 8/9 margin 0, 1 negative (`We Eat Rhythm` −0.03, two distinct wrong-octave-region candidates); `1x` 1 row (`Their Law` −0.079). **Only `Their Law` directly shows a literal true-1× candidate scoring higher than the octave the pipeline chose** — the single confirmed instance of the resolver flipping a winning correct tempo. The 13 zero-margin rows are consistent with, but do not prove, a resolver tie.

### Finding 3: GiantSteps is not octave-bound; its residual is genuine wrong-tempo

**Evidence:** `accuracy-forensics-giantsteps.json` → `errorTypeHistogram` = `{exact:537, other:98, threeHalf:10, twoThird:9, double:5, half:2}`; `recallSplit` = `{missTrueAtRank1:59, missTrueInTop5:104, missTrueAbsent:20}`.

**Detail:** Only **7/124** GS misses are octave (double+half). 98 are non-harmonic "other" (genuinely wrong tempo) + 19 triplet. Octave work moves GiantSteps by ≤7 tracks (≤1.1pp). Confidence reliability is cleanly monotonic on GS (0.5→70%, 0.8→91%, 0.9→94%) — DSP confidence is meaningful there.

## Deduced Conclusions

### Deduction 1: OA300's ceiling is octave-error-bound at the outcome level; the exact mechanism is unconfirmed

**Based on:** Findings 1 + 2 (with the Finding-2 margin caveat).

**Reasoning:** 16/24 misses are exact octaves and only 3/24 are generation-bound — so the *outcome* bottleneck is octave error, not candidate generation. What the artifacts do **not** yet prove is the *mechanism*: whether (a) the generator ranks the wrong octave at rank 1 and `resolveOctaveAmbiguity` never synthesizes/selects the true partner, or (b) both octaves are candidates and the resolver breaks a near-tie wrong. `Their Law` confirms (b) exists; the 13 zero-margin rows are unresolved between (a) and (b). Both mechanisms route through the same stage (`resolveOctaveAmbiguity`: sub-band vote + fixed `0.3`/`0.5` thresholds, weights `[0.5,1.0,1.5,2.0]`).

**Conclusion:** The highest-leverage OA300 lever is **octave-decision behavior**, proven at the category/ceiling level. **Octave-perfect ceiling = 58 + 16 = 74/82 (90.2%)** Acc1 (equals current Acc2, by construction — Acc2 already credits octave); +triplet → 76/82. The mechanism (rescore an existing 1× candidate vs synthesize-and-decide the partner) is **not** settled by these artifacts — see Missing Evidence.

### Deduction 2: Octave work is an OA300-specific lever; GiantSteps needs a different (harder) lever

**Based on:** Finding 3.

**Reasoning:** GS octave misses ≤7; residual is non-harmonic wrong-tempo (the ML/Epic-7 lane), not a decision the DSP octave logic can reach.

**Conclusion:** Phase 1 octave work should be measured primarily on OA300; do not expect GiantSteps lift. Keep GS as the no-regression guard.

## Hypothesized Paths

### Hypothesis 1: A periodicity-tie tiebreak (sub-band reweight OR beat-grid support) flips the 13 margin-0 octave misses

**Status:** Open

**Theory:** Because the two octaves tie on score (margin 0), a *small* correct tiebreak signal recovers them. Candidate stronger signals: (a) re-tuned sub-band vote weights/thresholds, (b) beat-grid-support rescoring (deferred W53/W74 — which octave the step-11 beat grid better fits).

**Would confirm:** An oracle octave-decision (or a flag-gated tiebreak) recovers ≥13 of 16 on OA300 with no GiantSteps regression.

**Would refute:** Re-tuning the sub-band vote moves the 16 by < a few, i.e. the tie is genuinely unresolvable from these features → escalates to a learned octave classifier (Epic 12 ML).

### Hypothesis 2: Candidate-rescoring alone (W53) is INSUFFICIENT for most octave misses

**Status:** Open — blocked on Missing Evidence

**Theory:** For the ~13 `matchedFactor=="2x"/"0.5x"` misses, the recall summary only proves a *harmonic* of truth is a candidate — possibly only the octave itself. If the true 1× is **not** a literal candidate, re-ranking the candidate pool can't reach it; the partner must be *synthesized* inside `resolveOctaveAmbiguity`. Only 3 misses show `matchedFactor=="1x"` (true value literally present).

**Would confirm:** Candidate-list dump shows the true 1× absent from the pool in the ×2/×0.5 misses → the support signal must feed the octave *decision*, not a candidate re-rank.

**Would refute:** True 1× is present (lower-ranked) in most → a candidate-rescore (W53) can recover them without touching `resolveOctaveAmbiguity`.

## Conclusion

**Confidence:** High for the *what* (octave-error-bound, 74/82 ceiling — arithmetic Codex-verified); Low/Medium for the *mechanism* (resolver-tie vs generator-ranks-octave), pending the candidate-dump + trace.

OA300 accuracy is **octave-error-bound at the outcome level**: 67% of misses are exact octaves, only 3/24 are generation-bound, and a truth harmonic is the top candidate in 15/16. Ceiling if the octave decision is fixed: **74/82**. The *mechanism* is not proven by these artifacts — `scoreMargin ≈ 0` is largely tautological for harmonic-matched rows (Codex), so only `Their Law` confirms the resolver flipping a higher-scored true tempo. GiantSteps is *not* octave-bound (≤7 misses) and serves as the no-regression guard. The open mechanism question — re-rank an existing 1× candidate (W53) vs synthesize-and-decide the octave (Epic 12) — hinges on whether the true 1× is a literal candidate, which the current artifacts don't record.

## Follow-up: 2026-06-28 — un-gate experiment REJECTED (empirical, reverted)

### What was tried
Per `spec-octave-resolver-symmetry.md`: un-gate `BPMAnalyzer.resolveOctaveAmbiguity` so the sub-band vote becomes AUTHORITATIVE + SYMMETRIC (honor a slow vote → demote `best` to the slower fundamental), anchored on `candidates[0]`, with a `bpmMin` demotion floor. FOUR variants benchmarked (two un-gate + two score-guarded). (Origin: an agy code-review claimed the faster-only ratchet was "a bug"; codex + the MIR-tiebreak framing concurred — a unanimous quorum.)

### Result — ALL FOUR demote variants FAIL (keep-or-revert → REVERTED to baseline 85932b3)
| Variant | OA300 Acc1 | GiantSteps Acc1 |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline (faster-only ratchet) | 58/82 | 537/661 |
| Un-gate, symmetric authoritative vote | 40/82 (−18) | 503/661 (−34) |
| Un-gate, strict-tie (demote only on strict slow win) | 39/82 (−19) | 507/661 (−30) |
| Score-guarded demote, threshold 0.8 | 54/82 (−4) | 534/661 (−3) |
| Score-guarded demote, threshold 0.95 | 56/82 (−2) | 536/661 (−1) |

Acc2 held/rose throughout (octave errors still credit under Acc2). Two findings: (1) strict-tie gave no improvement → the over-demotion is genuine STRICT slow wins, not ties. (2) The score-plausibility guard (only demote when the slow octave's fusion score is ≥ threshold × the fast candidate's — blocks the 0.70→0.009 ghost demote) cut the damage ~5× and the synthetic clicks stayed correct, but it **asymptotes to baseline from below** (0.8→−4/−3, 0.95→−2/−1, threshold→1.0 → baseline) and **never overshoots**. The empirical CEILING of any demote-based octave lever is therefore NEUTRAL, not a lift — the slow octave is genuinely score/ACF-competitive on enough correct-fast tracks (bar-level kick/bass repetition) that demoting breaks ≥ what it fixes at every operating point.

### Confirmed root cause — the asymmetry is LOAD-BEARING, not a bug
- Autocorrelation is structurally slow-biased: a signal periodic at `T` is also periodic at `2T`, so the subharmonic (slow-octave) lag autocorrelates ≥ the fundamental. Measured directly (per-band instrumentation): for a 160-BPM click the 80-BPM (slow) lag is **~25% STRICTLY stronger in ALL four sub-bands** (decisive 5.0–0.0 slow win, not a tie).
- The candidate generator already surfaces the subharmonic; the sub-band vote (`tie-favors-slow`) compounds it. The faster-only ratchet was COMPENSATING. Removing it demoted ~18 OA300 + ~34 GS correct fast tempos to their slow octave — often a near-zero-score "ghost" (the resolver demoted a 0.70-fusion-score anchor to a 0.009-score subharmonic candidate).

### Three models + the literature framing were all wrong — and conceded
codex + agy originally called the asymmetry "a bug to fix"; the MIR framing (octave = tiebreak/classifier) was cited in support. The empirical benchmark refuted it on BOTH corpora. In post-mortem BOTH models conceded: the asymmetry is load-bearing; `subBandVote` is NOT a trustworthy octave arbiter; the literature actually prescribes a perceptual prior / learned classifier, NOT a raw periodicity vote — we mis-applied it by trusting the hand-tuned vote. (Lesson: empirical benchmark > unanimous model consensus; the operator's revert-if-no-lift gate + the adversarial elicitation passes earned their keep.)

### DO NOT re-attempt — the whole DEMOTE class is exhausted
Demoting periodicity candidates to a slower octave is a DEAD END in EVERY form tried: un-gating the vote (symmetric/strict-tie) regresses ~−18/−34; the score-plausibility guard (even at 0.95) only asymptotes back to baseline, never lifts. The faster-only ratchet is intentional compensation for ACF subharmonic bias and must stay. The octave lever needs an arbiter that does NOT trust raw periodicity (ranked by near-term promise):
1. **Perceptual log-normal ~120 BPM resonance prior** as a TIEBREAK (not override) — literature-backed (Parncutt 1994 / Moelants & van Noorden 1999); the demote experiments did not test this.
2. **Beat-grid coherence** to validate candidate tempos (deferred W53/W74) — an independent signal, not periodicity.
3. **Learned octave classifier** (periodicity-vector + SVM/CNN) — Epic 12 / Gkiokas-style. The sub-band vote may serve as a weak FEATURE here, never as an authoritative rule.
