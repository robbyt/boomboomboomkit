## Successes

- **Story-creation discipline matured dramatically.** Every story past 3-3 included a numbered Key Design Decisions block at the top — directly honoring Epic 2's retro action item. Story 3-3 had 13 DDs; Story 3-6 went through 5 review cycles before kickoff (v1.1 → v1.5). The pre-implementation Codex Failure Mode Analysis on Story 3-5 caught 8 high, 12 medium, 5 low findings — and the *self-consistency* round caught 2 contradictions the FMA itself introduced. Multi-pass review is now load-bearing.

- **Metadata corroboration (Story 3-6) actually moved accuracy.** +1/+1 on OA300 from 5 tagged tracks (4 corroborated same-tempo, 1 uncorroborated). Default-on, byte-equality preserved when disabled, no GiantSteps regression (corpus has no embedded BPM tags). After four DSP-focused stories that all netted to zero, this was the breakthrough.

- **Typed-evidence pattern (Story 3-3b) closed a real correctness bug.** Two candidates with BPMs `61.04` and `60.95` would have collapsed into one trace entry under the `%.1f` key scheme. We promoted the pattern into a project-local Claude Skill (`.claude/skills/bpm-diagnostic-trace/`) with five audit grep recipes — `Sources/` and `Tests/` are now provably collision-free, and future trace fields have a checklist.

- **Disabled-policy byte-equality contracts held everywhere.** Every story with new behavior shipped with a paired test that proved opt-out is byte-identical to the pre-story pipeline. Story 3-6b refactored that test to call the same `runPreCorroborationPipeline` helper production uses — eliminated drift risk.

- **ADR-11 (Options-first) emerging mid-epic and being respected the rest of the way.** Story 3-3a introduced it; 3-4 (`durationHint`), 3-5 (`votingPolicy`/`votingThreshold`), 3-6 (`metadataPolicy`) all conformed without re-litigation.

- **Architecture invariants now have explicit regression tests.** `DSPTechnique.allCases.count == 7`, `TechniqueSet.allDSPCombinations().count == 128`, `MetadataSource.allCases.count == 3`, `optimal.contains(.clickTrackCorrelation) == false`, `CandidateMergeStrategy.allCases.count == 8` — all unit-test-locked. Future refactors that flip them by accident fail at unit-test time, not corpus benchmark time.

- **Honest impact reporting became the cultural norm.** The per-track click-impact report (Story 3-3) and duration-impact report (Story 3-4) revealed both techniques are functionally near-inert at default intensity (click changes 1/82 tracks, duration changes 0/82). We shipped that finding plainly in completion notes rather than hand-waving. That honesty is the most valuable cultural artifact of this epic.

- **Wall-clock improved 19%** as a side-effect of multiple stories. Never claimed in the epic title but real: mean per-track analysis dropped from 0.216 s to 0.175 s on M5 Max.

- **VotingPolicy sweep discovery (`.thresholdGated@0.50`)** beats `.simpleMajority` by +1 Acc2 on OA300. Kept default conservative per empirical-conservatism rationale, but the substrate now lets future stories revisit the default with corpus evidence.

- **Pre-implementation Codex multi-pass review caught real critical bugs before code landed.** Story 3-3 kernel-pre-reverse direction inversion, Story 3-5 DD#3 vs Task 2.5 contradiction, Story 3-6 architectural cascade across 41 call sites. Without these passes, all three would have shipped as bugs.

## Challenges

- **Net DSP improvement on default corpora was small.** Of the +1/+1 gain on OA300, all came from Story 3-6 metadata corroboration. The five DSP-focused stories (3-1 trace-only, 3-2 quadratic-fit hybrid, 3-3 click rescore, 3-4 duration hint, 3-5 voting policy) collectively moved the corpus by 0 tracks at default config. The 128-combo ablation revealed why: max single-window Acc1 across all combinations is 55/82 (67.1%) — **the corpus is at its DSP information-theoretic ceiling for the default configuration.** "Epic 3: DSP Accuracy Improvement" was a misleading title for what was largely an architectural-foundation epic.

- **Story 3-1 had to pivot mid-flight from behavioral-fix to trace-only** because Acc1 regressed on DnB. Result: AC #4 deferred. The named target (Charly @ 106.2 instead of 160) and three more DnB triplet pairs (Faraday_Bunker, Yin Yang, HEFT_Anagram 6) still misdetect by -33%.

- **Story 3-2 Icicle (the named exemplar) regressed from 0.1 → 0.3 BPM.** Story self-marked AC #3 as PARTIAL and shipped. Within 2% Acc1 tolerance so corpus accuracy holds, but the headline track this story exists to fix got *worse* by 0.2 BPM in the absolute. Tracked in `deferred-work.md` as known trade-off.

- **Story 3-4 HALTED mid-dev when GiantSteps regressed at default values.** Resolved by adding 180s minimum-file-seconds threshold — which suppresses the hint corpus-wide on GiantSteps (all clips <180s) and renders it inert on OA300 (`changedFinalBPM=0`). We essentially shipped a feature whose default config does nothing on either benchmark corpus. Story explicitly recommends keeping it on as "cheap insurance" — but insurance against what, if no benefit was ever measured?

- **Original Story 3-3a draft was Codex-rejected with "Block — re-author" verdict.** Required complete rewrite. Story scope ballooning into a kitchen-sink refactor is a recurring risk; Story 3-5 also had two self-introduced contradictions that only the second-pass review caught.

- **96 kHz × 126 BPM is a real wrong-BPM result at a supported sample rate** (detected ≈125.35 vs expected 126.0 — confirmed live). No corpus impact (OA300/GiantSteps are 44.1 k) so it's invisible in benchmarks. Tracked in `deferred-work.md` as Story 3-7; promote when consumer signal or 96 k corpus fixture forces the issue.

- **Story 3-2's review section had 15 stale `[ ]` checkboxes** of which 10 were quietly fixed by Stories 3-3 / 3-3a / 3-3b but never checked off. The artifact misled retrospective analysis and pointed at an "imaginary punch list." Cleaned up in this retro (D1).

- **Onset-envelope quality on heavily-mastered material is the upstream weakness** the click rescore and duration hint can't compensate for. Clipped/limited DnB has flatter onset peaks, which makes onset detection fail before any later step can rescore. The 4 named DnB triplet failures likely have this as a contributing cause, not just the disambiguation step. Worth flagging for Epic 4 ML feature-engineering.

## Key Insights

1. **The DSP single-window Acc1 ceiling on OA300 is 55/82 (67.1%).** Documented in 128-combo ablation. The default config reaches 58/82 because of multi-window aggregation + merge strategy + metadata corroboration doing work above the per-window ceiling. Any future "DSP accuracy improvement" story at default intensity must either (a) change architecture (top-K refinement, post-disambiguation merge), (b) target a different intensity, or (c) operate on a different signal (file metadata, ML features). Pure technique-tuning in the existing 7-case `DSPTechnique` family has reached saturation.

2. **Multi-pass pre-implementation review is now load-bearing.** Codex (or equivalent) Failure Mode Analysis BEFORE code lands has caught real critical bugs that would have shipped as silent correctness failures. The cost is high (Story 3-5 had 3 review passes, ~30 findings each); the cost of skipping it would have been higher.

3. **Story-file `[ ]` checkboxes go stale when later stories address findings without checking them off.** `deferred-work.md` is the authoritative ledger; story files capture point-in-time review state and don't get retroactively edited. Process change: cross-reference + check off when later stories address prior items.

4. **Honest inertness reporting is more valuable than optimistic completion notes.** Per-track impact reports (Story 3-3, 3-4) and the corroboration tolerance sweep (Story 3-6) shipped findings that could have been hand-waved. They reveal where techniques actually move the needle vs where they're decorative. This pattern must continue into Epic 4.

5. **The accuracy-affecting work (Story 3-2 GiantSteps +1/+1, Story 3-6 OA300 +1/+1) operated on signal axes that the other five DSP stories didn't touch** — sub-BPM precision and file-level metadata. The "let's add another DSP technique to the existing pipeline" pattern is exhausted.

Epic 3 delivered 10 stories with zero accuracy regression and +1/+1 net gains on both OA300 and GiantSteps. Test count nearly doubled (157 → 307). Wall-clock improved 19%. Public API surface tripled with 5 typed-evidence structs, 4 new public types, and 3 Options fields plus the mlTechnique slot reservation for Epic 4.

The empirical truth of the epic is harder than the headline: **of 10 stories, only 2 (Story 3-2 fine-grid, Story 3-6 metadata) actually moved corpus tracks.** The other 8 shipped working features that pass the "no regression" bar without changing benchmark numbers at default config. Story 3-3 click rescore changes 1/82 OA300 final BPMs; Story 3-4 duration hint changes 0/82.

**The retrospective surfaced this honestly** because Stories 3-3 and 3-4 instituted per-track impact reports as a cultural artifact. That honesty is the most valuable foundation Epic 3 leaves for Epic 4: don't ship a feature that benchmarks don't see; don't claim what the impact report doesn't support; and don't conflate "feature works + tests pass" with "user-visible improvement." Epic 4's numeric delta gates (A1) bake this lesson into per-story success criteria.

The Charly/DnB triplet class — 4 OA300 tracks at -33% error — represents +4 Acc1 tracks (58 → 62 = 75.6%) of headroom that DSP cannot recover without architectural change. Story 4.5 (BNNS) inherits these as named regression targets. If ML can resolve ≥2 of them, the architectural investment pays for itself; if not, Epic 4 needs honest re-scoping like Epic 3's title did.
