## Shipped on 2026-10-17, decoder and selector revised 2026-10-18, decoder evidence rule revised 2026-10-19

- `Sources/BoomBoomBoomKit/FeatureSubstrate/Framewise/FramewiseObservations.swift`: the per-frame beat/downbeat probability value type and the `FramewiseObservationProducer` internal protocol, with explicit frame rate, origin, coverage window, and model and frontend identity. One timing statement: `timeForFrame(i)` is the instant frame `i` represents, and `firstFrameOriginSeconds == timeForFrame(0)`. Probability semantics: beat probability `B` is inclusive (a downbeat is also a beat) and `D` is the marginal downbeat probability, with `0 <= D <= B <= 1`. The initializer enforces the whole contract with one precondition each: every value in both arrays is finite and in `[0, 1]`, and `D <= B` at every frame (bounds check added 2026-10-19; every fixture, and the stub's max-normalized `B` clamped to `[0, 1]` with `D = 0.5 * B`, already satisfy it, so no fixture changed). The compatible categorical decomposition is `nonbeat = 1 - B`, `ordinaryBeat = B - D`, `downbeat = D`; a reference model's `1 - B - D` assumes exclusive classes and must not be applied to these channels. Coverage in frames: `usableFrameCount` converts `coverageWindowSeconds * frameRate` to an integer, rounding to the nearest integer when within `1e-6` frames of it and flooring otherwise, clipped to `[0, frameCount]`; the decoder reads only frames below it. The plain floor it replaced (2026-10-19) lost a valid trailing frame to binary roundoff: the stub reports coverage as `frameCount * hopSize / sampleRate`, and for 29 frames at 44.1 kHz with hop 441, `29 * 441 / 44100 * 100` evaluates to `28.999999999999996`, which floors to 28. A genuinely fractional window still floors (0.285 s at 100 fps is 28 frames).
- `Sources/BoomBoomBoomKit/FeatureSubstrate/Framewise/DSPStubObservationProducer.swift`: DSP-derived stub producer (`dsp-stub.v1`, frontend `bpmanalyzer-onset.v1`) routing `BPMAnalyzer.computeMelOnsetEnvelope` through a max normalizer. Its origin absorbs the Story 15.3 frame-time convention, so `timeForFrame` already matches `BeatGridAnalyzer`. The stub is the falsifier called out in DD #0: a decision-worthy baseline, not placeholder infrastructure.
- `Sources/BoomBoomBoomKit/FeatureSubstrate/Framewise/BarStateDecoder.swift`: the 4/4 structured decoder (`decoderIdentifier` `bar-state-decoder.v2.local-evidence`, carried on every candidate). Scope-cut vs the full Böck ISMIR 2016 Viterbi: a windowed peak-pick with a local evidence rule and a bounded phase correction. Revised on 2026-10-19 after review found that one positive activation meant both "a beat was observed" and "move the clock here", so a positive floor emitted a beat at every position and each gap position re-anchored about a quarter period early. The source for the revision is `../rekordboxReader/docs/boomboomboomkit-beat-strength-and-ml-guidance.md` (sections "Answers to the four questions" and "Probability semantics before introducing an HMM"). Each predicted position takes three distinct decisions:
  1. *Prediction.* A floating-point clock advances by one period `P` (in frames) at every position and `gridIndex` increments at every position. The decoder never changes the rate; the fitter estimates it.
  2. *Observation, two tiers.* Inside the on-window `[p - P/4, p + P/4)`, the correction tier `[p - 0.15P, p + 0.15P)` (`maxPhaseCorrectionFraction`) is searched first; its argmax (ties: nearest to `p`, then earlier) is the observed beat if it passes the evidence rule. Otherwise the argmax of the rest of the on-window is the observed beat if it passes. If nothing passes, the position is prediction-only: no beat, the clock coasts, the off-window stays on `p`.
  3. *Correction.* A correction-tier observation moves the clock to `frame + P` (`phaseCorrectionGain` 1.0). An outer-tier observation is emitted with `correctedClock == false` and the clock coasts to `p + P`.

  *Evidence rule* (initial values, owned by a later tuning story): the background is the beat probabilities within `backgroundWindowPeriods = 4` periods on each side of `p`, excluding the on-window and clipped to the usable span; `background` is their median and `scale` their raw median absolute deviation; `effectiveScale = max(scale, minEffectiveScale = 0.01)`; a frame passes when `value - background >= prominenceMultiplier (3) * effectiveScale`, inclusive. A constant stream has prominence 0. **Quiet-train limit:** the stub normalizes by the span maximum and the minimum passing prominence is 0.03, so a beat train quieter than 3 percent of the loudest onset in the span is not observed (a 0.02 train with one 1.0 transient acquires only the transient; a 0.05 train is fully observed). An evidence scale independent of span normalization is a follow-up. Seed acquisition uses the same rule: admissible frames (`f + 3P/4 <= usableFrames`) are walked one period at a time and the first window whose argmax passes supplies the seed, so a quiet leading floor cannot choose a false initial phase; the seed frame is the first beat, with no on-window re-search, and a stronger inadmissible neighbour cannot displace it.

  *Provisional reacquisition:* when the previous position was prediction-only (a coasted outer-tier observation does not count) and the current observation is outer-tier, the decoder re-anchors on it tentatively and evaluates the next position under that provisional clock. The reacquisition is confirmed only when that position yields an observation within `reacquisitionConfirmFraction = 0.05` of a period of the provisional prediction; then both beats report `correctedClock == true` and `reacquisitionCount` increments. Otherwise the provisional evaluation is discarded, the next position is evaluated once under the coasted clock, and the observation stays an ordinary outer-tier beat; the same happens when coverage ends before the confirming position exists. **Stated limit:** a stub-era heuristic with one position of lookahead. A second transient one period after the first confirms a wrong reacquisition (pinned by `decoderConfirmsWrongReacquisitionOnTwoAlignedTransients`: the clock stays 10 frames late and the real clicks from 310 on are emitted outer-tier; against the Story 15.7 fitter at its pre-triage head that fixture still fits 120 BPM with keptFraction 9/11). A gap long enough for the clock error to exceed a quarter period can displace grid indices by one, which reacquisition does not repair. Follow-up 4 owns a real reacquisition hypothesis.

  *Scoring:* every scored position adds the full on-window maximum minus the off-window maximum, whichever tier supplied the observation and whether or not one passed; the off-window `[a + P/4, a + 3P/4)` anchors on the committed clock decision `a` (the corrected frame, or `p`), never on a discarded peak, and a rolled-back position is scored once. Decoding stops before any position whose prediction or committed anchor would put its off-window past the usable span, so every predicted position is scored on complete windows.

  *No evidence versus inadmissible evidence:* the all-zero span, coverage, and period guards return nil as before. When no admissible seed window passes, the whole usable span is scanned: if no frame passes the evidence rule (a positive constant, a subthreshold ramp), the result is a no-evidence candidate (`evidence == .none`, no beats, positions counted from frame 0 at the candidate period as a bookkeeping phase that is not an acquired musical phase, signed contrast over those positions, `meanBeatSupport == 0`, `downbeatPhaseUnique == false`, `reacquisitionCount == 0`); if some frame passes but none in an admissible seed window, the result is nil.

  *Beats and downbeats:* each beat carries `gridIndex`, raw `beatSupport`, `prominence` (probability units above the local background, evidence and not a probability), and `correctedClock`. The downbeat phase is the bar position (0 to 3) with the highest MEAN downbeat support, ties to the lowest populated phase; `downbeatPhaseUnique` is true only for a strict unique winner. The mean removes sample-count bias and nothing more, and on the stub's `0.5 * beat` channel the phase follows beat amplitude, not bar evidence. The candidate carries `contrastScore` (on-beat minus off-beat support averaged over every predicted position) and `predictedPositionCount`.
- `Sources/BoomBoomBoomKit/FeatureSubstrate/Framewise/MetricalLevelSelector.swift`: runs the decoder at `tempoBPM`, its half, and its double, and returns the level with the highest `contrastScore` (reported per level as `scoreByLevel`, with chosen and runner-up candidates). Ties resolve toward `.quarter`. Selection requires a seed-level candidate as the comparison baseline, so the result is nil when the seed level does not decode. A no-evidence quarter candidate is a valid baseline: on a positive constant every level scores 0 and the tie keeps `.quarter`, but on a nonconstant stream without evidence the signed contrast can favour another level (the 0.01 to 0.03 ramp picks `.double`); by design this rejects a decodable half or double candidate when only that level fits the coverage (a caller wanting a result there must seed at the finer tempo). The first version ranked by mean on-beat support, which cannot separate octaves and, combined with a decoder that stepped by a fractional period without re-anchoring, picked `.half` at every tempo whose period is not a whole number of hops (124 to 174 BPM).

Summary: Internal framewise observation and 4/4 decoder infrastructure for Epic 12 corrected-ranking item 7(b). A decoded audio span becomes timed beat and downbeat observations, a bar-state 4/4 decoder returns a candidate beat sequence, and a constant-tempo refinement locks the grid. All kept separate from `MLTechnique` and from the `SignalSource.beatGrid` placeholder. Epic 15 ships the plumbing and a DSP-derived stub producer; Epic 14 (gated on Epic 12 Gate 1) owns bundling a trained observation weight.

## Key Design Decisions

0. **Stub-plus-decoder is a decision-worthy baseline, not placeholder infrastructure.** The DSP-derived stub observation producer and the 4/4 structured decoder together are a *falsifier* for Epic 14's eventual training question. If the stub-plus-decoder path already beats `BeatGridAnalyzer` on OA300 and GiantSteps against the Phase 9 reference set, Epic 14 has evidence for training a trained observation head; if it does not, Epic 14 saves the retrain. This story is cheap compared to the training round it informs.
1. **New internal interface, not on MLTechnique.** Add an `internal` protocol in `Sources/BoomBoomBoomKit/` that takes a decoded audio span and returns a value type carrying timed beat and downbeat observations. The scalar `MLTechnique.evaluate(trace:) -> MLEvaluation?` surface at `Sources/BoomBoomBoomKit/MLTechnique.swift:143` is preserved; no public API changes this story.
2. **Observations are distinct from any final playable grid.** The observation value type carries per-frame beat and downbeat probabilities, the frame rate, the first-frame origin against the decoded span, the coverage window in seconds, and a model and frontend identifier pair. It does not expose a beat sequence; a separate decoder consumes it. Frame timing follows the fixed-hop convention already used by `MLFeatureFrames` rather than the whole-segment-to-512-frame rescaling at `Sources/BoomBoomBoomKitML/BNNSTechnique.swift:537`.
3. **Published method, independently trained weights, stub producer in this story.** The decoder follows the Böck et al. ISMIR 2016 family through the madmom reference (bar-state HMM over neural beat and downbeat observations with Viterbi decoding). No dependency on rekordbox weights or the TensorFlow bundle under `rekordbox.app/Contents/Resources/models/detect_beat/`. This story ships a DSP-derived stub producer conforming to the protocol so the end-to-end path integrates without a bundled model; trained weights arrive in a follow-up that owns its own evaluation gate.
4. **DSP path is baseline and fallback, not modified.** The new path is evaluated alongside `BeatGridAnalyzer` (`Sources/BoomBoomBoomKit/BeatGridAnalyzer.swift:49`). When the observation producer returns an empty result or coverage below the configured minimum, the pipeline returns the existing `BeatGridAnalyzer` output byte-identically. The DSP path's code is unchanged in this story.
5. **No circular confidence into BPM.** The derived grid does not vote back into BPM selection. `SignalSource.beatGrid` at `Sources/BoomBoomBoomKit/SignalPool/SignalSource.swift:12` and `SignalWeights.beatGrid` at `Sources/BoomBoomBoomKit/SignalPool/SignalWeights.swift:37` remain placeholders; this story adds no producer and does not change the weight. The ensemble is not touched.
6. **Compare musical half and double tempo before locking a grid.** The decoder returns a candidate beat sequence annotated with a metrical level. A metrical-level selector evaluates the candidate at that level and at half and double, picks the level with the highest per-beat observation support over the coverage window, then emits the final constant-tempo grid. The chosen level, runner-up level, and both support scores are written to `BPMDiagnosticTrace` so the choice is auditable.
7. **Benchmark before any bundled weight.** A model is not bundled solely because the inference path runs. The observation producer stays stub-only in this story. Any later weight is introduced in a separate story that measures accuracy against the Phase 9 matched evaluation harness; `BoomBoomBoomKitML` continues to build without a bundled downbeat weight file.
8. **Coordinate with Epic 12 corrected-ranking item 7(b) (standing ownership rule).** Epic 12 charter item 7(b) names a multi-task beat and tempo head as a candidate octave arbiter (`_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/epics.md:2393`). Epic 15 Story 15.6 ships the independent observation and decoder path with a stub producer. The fusion decision (whether the derived grid votes into primary BPM selection via `SignalSource.beatGrid`, and under what weight) stays with Epic 12. The trained-weight follow-up for the stub producer stays with Epic 14 (dormant until Epic 12 Gate 1 fires); Epic 15 does not open a Story 15.6.1 for training on its own.

## Open questions for the PRD

- Which observation frame rate the protocol standardizes on. The published method and the rekordbox native decoder both use 100 fps, while the current `MLFeatureFrames` substrate is fixed-hop at a hop configured per model. The protocol must declare a frame rate rather than assume one, and the stub needs a canonical choice.
- How the coverage window is defined relative to leading and trailing silence. The decoder expects observations spanning the usable segment; the stub's coverage must line up with how `BeatGridCoverage` already reports usable span, or the mismatch surfaces as a fallback trigger on real tracks.
- How the metrical-level selector reports its decision when the two candidates are within observation noise, including whether a minimum support margin or an abstain signal is required before a non-default level is emitted.
- Where trained observation weights will come from in the follow-up story. The Böck ISMIR 2016 reference and the madmom public implementation describe the training recipe; the project has no retrained weights in this story and the follow-up's own evaluation gate must be defined before any weight is bundled.
- Whether `BeatGrid.coverage` or an adjacent type needs a new field for the chosen metrical level and runner-up, or whether the full report lives only on `BPMDiagnosticTrace`. The AC 7 constraint is that `BeatGridAnalyzer`'s output shape does not change in this story; the trace is the current carrier.
